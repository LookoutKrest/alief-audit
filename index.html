The Alief Audit

Do you treat AI the way you think about it?

Standing on a glass floor over the Grand Canyon, you believe the glass will hold you. Your legs are not so sure. The philosopher Tamar Gendler called that gut-level conviction an alief: something you hold to be true in your reflexes that you deny in your reasoning.

Most of us now have an alief about AI. This audit measures two things: what you believe about AI, and how you treat it. Then it shows you where the two meet, and where they don't.

14 questions. About two minutes. No login. Nothing you answer leaves your browser.

A companion to the article AI Consciousness: The Alief Thesis by Ed Daniels.

This is a mirror, not a diagnosis. It is not a validated psychological test, and the scoring is one writer's judgment about which habits signal belief and which signal alief. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere.
